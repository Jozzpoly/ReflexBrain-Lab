# Pre-O0 Sociality and Independent Causality Deep Dive — 2026-10-06

Status: **FOUNDATIONAL OPEN QUESTION · SECOND ACTOR NOT PROMOTED**

## Core question

Does a first serious ReflexBrain organism require another autonomous actor to generate qualitatively rich actor-relative meaning, or can an independently changing non-social world provide enough causal pressure?

The question is not:
"are two agents more fun?"

It is:
> what new causal distinctions become possible only when another trajectory is itself adaptive, private and self-generated?

---

# 1. Separate levels of independent causality

## S0 — one actor, mostly static world

World changes mainly because actor acts.

Useful for:
- body/controller;
- basic sensorimotor contingencies.

Weak for:
- stale knowledge;
- self/world distinction;
- unexpected events;
- actor-relative meaning.

## S1 — one actor + autonomous non-cognitive process

Examples:
- moving mechanism;
- topology changer;
- transport process;
- material transformation;
- field drift.

Useful for:
- exogenous causality;
- hidden change;
- prediction mismatch;
- stale memory;
- timing/active perception.

No second perspective exists.

## S2 — one actor + simple embodied autonomous body

The other body has its own local policy but little/no private memory.

Useful for:
- collision/avoidance;
- non-scripted moving obstruction;
- following/chasing/spacing dynamics.

Still weak for:
- differentiated private histories.

## S3 — two actors with private history

Each actor:
- perceives partially;
- remembers differently;
- has its own ongoing organization.

Now the same World event can legitimately mean different things to each actor.

## S4 — social interpretation/communication

Only later:
- signaling;
- commitments;
- norms;
- communication;
- inferred intentions.

Do not jump to S4.

---

# 2. What another actor uniquely adds

### reciprocal causality

The other actor reacts to me while I react to it.

The causal relation is closed across two autonomous loops.

### private asymmetry

Two actors can occupy the same World while:
- seeing different things;
- remembering different events;
- having different body states;
- pursuing different ongoing organizations.

### contingent response

The other body may not follow a fixed mechanical trajectory.

My action changes what it does next.

### interaction history

A relation can depend on prior encounters.

This creates naturally actor-relative history without authoring a semantic "relationship score".

### distributed world activity

Meaningful events can occur while Owner and focal actor are elsewhere.

This strongly matches the long-term "world continues elsewhere" aspiration.

---

# 3. What sociality can fake

Two moving entities are extremely easy to anthropomorphize.

Apparent:
- greeting;
- avoidance;
- following;
- turn-taking;
- cooperation;

may emerge from:
- collision avoidance;
- matched speed;
- geometry;
- simple attraction/repulsion;
- periodic routines.

This is scientifically useful but must not be overclaimed.

The perceptual-crossing literature is a good warning:
interaction dynamics themselves can create reliable coordination even when individual discrimination is weak.

Therefore:

> emergent dyadic behavior is evidence about coupling, not automatically evidence about social understanding.

# 4. Critical controls inspired by perceptual crossing

## SD1 — live partner vs replayed partner

Record one partner trajectory.

Compare focal actor interacting with:
- live responsive partner;
- identical replayed trajectory that cannot react.

Question:
does reciprocal responsiveness materially change behavior?

If not, apparent sociality may be simple tracking.

## SD2 — actor vs non-agent physical twin

Same body/motion statistics.

One is driven by autonomous controller;
one by scripted/mechanical process.

Can focal behavior differ only after lawful evidence of responsiveness?

Do not label either to the actor.

## SD3 — partner absent

Remove second actor while keeping environmental dynamics.

What organism phenomena disappear?

This tells us whether sociality is foundational or merely enriching.

## SD4 — private-history swap

Keep current visible state matched.

Swap/ablate focal actor's encounter history.

Can later interaction differ for a causal private-history reason?

## SD5 — partner-memory ablation

Researcher removes the partner's private memory only.

Does dyadic trajectory change first through partner decision, then World, then focal private evidence?

Useful first-divergence test.

## SD6 — symmetric bodies, asymmetric histories

Same morphology/policy.

Different experience histories.

Do roles/behavior differentiate without authored identity roles?

Strong future test.

# 5. Social affordances should not be labels

Do not expose:
- friend;
- enemy;
- partner;
- blocking actor;
- cooperative;
- hostile;
- wants X.

First social percept may only be:
- embodied track;
- motion;
- contact;
- perhaps sound/action events.

Any later "this actor tends to yield/help/block" should derive from history or higher cognition.

---

# 6. Interaction itself may become a dynamical system

Enactive/social interaction work emphasizes that interaction can develop its own momentum and constraints while participants remain autonomous.

Useful research implication:

A dyadic encounter may have a state that is not reducible to:
- actor A internal state;
- actor B internal state;

because reciprocal coupling changes both in real time.

Examples:
- mutual circling;
- turn-taking;
- following/leading transitions;
- reciprocal obstruction;
- shared manipulation.

Do not immediately create an explicit "interaction state" object.

First observe whether such a phenomenon exists in the coupled traces.

---

# 7. One actor may still be the better first microscope

Reasons to remain at S1 initially:

- cleaner causal attribution;
- easier private-history controls;
- easier body/perception debugging;
- lower anthropomorphism;
- isolates self/world distinction.

Reasons to move to S3 earlier:

- single-actor ecology may never generate enough actor-relative ambiguity;
- another private trajectory naturally creates meaningful unseen events;
- same-event/different-history pressure appears without semantic fixture design;
- aligns with long-term SPC/Feniks worlds.

No decision yet.

---

# 8. Strong discriminative comparison

Instead of choosing socially by taste:

Build later, if needed, two matched worlds:

### World D — dynamic mechanism
one focal actor + autonomous non-cognitive mover/process.

### World A — autonomous peer
replace the mechanism with a simple private-history actor while matching gross movement/contact statistics as much as possible.

Ask:

- Does private peer adaptivity create qualitatively new focal history?
- Does reciprocal coupling produce new stable/unstable interaction structures?
- Do new ReflexBrain-relevant ambiguities appear?
- Or does most value already come from independent exogenous causality?

This directly answers whether second actor is foundational.

---

# 9. Owner role in social ecology

Owner should not impersonate the second actor as the primary source of social pressure.

Owner may:
- observe;
- perturb environment;
- occasionally intervene.

The peer must continue its own trajectory without Owner.

Otherwise R2 clicker returns socially.

---

# 10. Current judgement

Second actor is **not required yet**, but sociality is too important to defer as a vague future feature.

Promote it to a foundational comparison axis.

Strongest sequence currently:

1. qualify one actor in a dynamic independent ecology (S1);
2. compare against a simple autonomous embodied peer (S2/S3) under matched mechanics;
3. only promote sociality if reciprocal private causality adds phenomena that non-agent dynamics cannot.

This preserves causal clarity while keeping the most promising long-term pressure close enough to test early.
