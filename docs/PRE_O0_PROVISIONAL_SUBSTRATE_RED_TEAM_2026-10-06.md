# Attack 1 — "dynamic ecology" can be scripted theatre

A moving mechanism, gate and topology changer may produce:
- off-screen change;
- occlusion;
- multiple routes;

while still being a tiny deterministic theme park whose only challenge is learning its authored rules.

Failure mode:
we congratulate the organism for living in a world that is effectively a puzzle clock.

## Required defense

Independent processes should be:
- lawful;
- perturbable;
- compositional;
- not reducible to scene-global phase;
- capable of interacting with actor/material state.

At least one process should have trajectory/state affected by local physical interaction, not just time.

Verdict:
**defensible if ecology is interaction-dependent rather than scheduled.**

# Attack 2 — P1 tracklets may smuggle objecthood

Temporary tokens already assert:
"there is one coherent entity here."

That may be more ontology than some sensorimotor theories would grant.

Failure mode:
future memory/learning gets object permanence almost for free.

## Required defense

Tracklet is a **perceptual scaffold**, not a World claim.

It must:
- be lossy;
- be allowed to split/merge/expire;
- never equal World ID;
- expose only local perceptual continuity;
- face hidden-swap/ambiguity controls.

P0 raw/local evidence remains available under it.

Verdict:
**acceptable V1 scaffold if replaceable and fallible.**

Not ontologically neutral.

# Attack 3 — C-A persistent controller may be L0 with better documentation

An option/controller can contain:
- hidden phase;
- target;
- progress;
- recovery;

and recreate exactly the scripted task architecture we rejected.

## Required defense

The controller's causal state must be interrogated.

Ask:
- if debug labels disappear, does behavior remain?
- do state transitions derive from private evidence?
- does it operate across rearranged layouts?
- can the same controller fail in understandable ways outside authored scene?
- can progress be expressed without hidden global target distance?

Verdict:
**still risky**.

C-A is selected only as diagnostic baseline because its failure is legible.

Supporting T0/T1/T2 can lead to:
- interfaces;
- plugins;
- generic motivation API;
- universal memory model;
- scenario runner.

This repeats R3 apparatus growth.

## Required defense

Choose **one first concrete specimen**.

The other hypotheses remain paper controls.

Reuse substrate only after second specimen actually exists.

Verdict:
**provisional substrate cannot be built until first occupant is chosen.**

This is the largest current blocker.

# Attack 7 — E0 controller + disc may impoverish all later observations

If body remains round:
- orientation-dependent affordance is weak;
- passage/morphology relations collapse;
- perception/action coupling may be poorer than intended.

## Required defense

Body boundary must allow B0/B1 very early.

Question:
should B1 be first rather than A/B later?

Arguments for B1 first:
- orientation already matters to actuation;
- asymmetric collision makes that visible/material;
- richer body-scaled affordance at little conceptual cost.

Arguments for B0 first:
- mechanically qualified;
- isolates ecology/perception;
- less contact pathology.

Verdict:
**V1 unresolved, not blocker.**

# Attack 8 — synthetic perception may remove sensory uncertainty too cleanly

Even P1 with exact bearing/range may make localization/search trivial.

Do not add artificial noise for realism.

Prefer:
- egocentric;
- local;
- occluded;
- relative;
over:
- global precise pose.

Verdict:
**manageable implementation boundary.**

A fixed controller with tracklets/memory may look coherent but never changes competence.

Failure mode:

- memory-dependent behavior;
or
- adaptive competence.

Do not call former learning.

Verdict:

# Attack 11 — visual legibility could still be too poor

A causally rich invisible state machine is not Owner-observable.

Persistent Playground's value partly came from being directly watchable.

## Required defense

World processes, occlusion, body orientation, carried/contact relations and local interactions must be visually legible.

Do not equate legibility with polish.

The Owner should be able to infer enough causal structure from the surface to form hypotheses.

Verdict:
**important product/research requirement.**

# Attack 12 — "maximum information gain" can become sterile science

Choosing the cleanest specimen can optimize experimental attribution while producing an organism too trivial to reveal emergent problems.

## Required defense

First occupant should be:
- simple enough to understand;
- rich enough to run for minutes;
- exposed to independent causal intersections;
- capable of surprising failure.

Not just one isolated unit test.

Verdict:
**selection criterion must include phenomenon richness, not only cleanliness.**

# Red-team result

Most substrate choices survive as reversible scaffolds.

One blocker remains:

> **There is still no chosen first occupant / trajectory source.**

Therefore implementation remains paused.

The next decision should not ask:
"which theory of life is true?"

Ask:

> Which first occupant gives the highest-value conjunction of:
> - actor-owned ongoing trajectory,
> - causal interpretability,
> - history dependence,
> - rich interaction with dynamic ecology,
> - potential to expose later semantic pressure,
> - low risk of fake-life pathology?

Candidates:
- T0 authored concern control;
- T1 developmental competence explorer;
- T2 adaptive habit.

A fourth possibility should be considered before choosing:

> **T0+ — authored concern as a weak anchor, while ordinary activity/exploration is not fully determined by the concern.**

This is not yet promoted.
It may provide trajectory without turning the whole organism into task execution.

But it risks hybridizing before components are understood.

Next:
compare these first-occupant options specifically as **research specimens**, not general motivation theories.
